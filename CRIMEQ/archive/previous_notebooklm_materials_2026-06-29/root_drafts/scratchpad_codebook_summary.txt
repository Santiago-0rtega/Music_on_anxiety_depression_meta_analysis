PROPOSED IMPROVED MUSIC CRIME-Q CODEBOOK
========================================

Total Items: 20 (reduced from 23 by removing non-applicable items)

Construct Breakdown:
  - QoR (Quality of Reporting): 9 items
  - MQ (Methodological Quality): 2 items  
  - RoB (Risk of Bias): 9 items

KEY IMPROVEMENTS:
1. Removed items 2X & 2Y (bench-top/lab work) - NOT applicable to music behavioral studies
2. Added music-specific examples and guidance throughout
3. Each item now has 5 detailed components:
   - Clear criteria for each response option
   - Music study examples
   - Common pitfalls to avoid
   - Assessment level (Study-level assessment, not cohort-level)

NEW STRUCTURE FOR ASSESSMENT FILE:
- Study-level assessment (one row per 20 studies)
- For each CRIME-Q item: 3 columns
  * Column 1: Assessment score (Yes/No/Partly/Unclear/NA)
  * Column 2: Justification (why you gave this score)
  * Column 3: Verbatim (direct quote from paper + page number)

Example columns for one item (5X - Music intervention reporting):
  5X_music_intervention_reporting_SCORE
  5X_music_intervention_reporting_JUSTIFICATION
  5X_music_intervention_reporting_VERBATIM

Total assessment columns: 20 items × 3 columns = 60 columns + Study_ID + Title = 62 columns

1X - Peer review
3X - Animals: reporting
3Y - Animals: technical quality  
3Z - Selection bias: baseline characteristics
4Y - Sample-size calculation
5X - Music intervention: reporting
5Y - Music intervention: technical quality
5Z(1) - Selection bias: sequence generation

9X - Discussion: limitations

10Z - Other bias: funder influence

NOTEBOOKLM INSTRUCTION TEMPLATE:

"For each CRIME-Q item in the paper:

1. SCORE: Assess the paper and assign: Yes, No, Partly, Unclear, or NA
   (Use the detailed criteria provided)

2. JUSTIFICATION: Explain WHY you gave this score (1-2 sentences)
   Example: "The authors reported strain and sex but not age/stage; therefore Partly"

3. VERBATIM: Provide a direct quote from the paper that supports this score
   Include page number(s) or section name
   Example: [page 3, Methods] 'Male Sprague-Dawley rats (n=24)'"
