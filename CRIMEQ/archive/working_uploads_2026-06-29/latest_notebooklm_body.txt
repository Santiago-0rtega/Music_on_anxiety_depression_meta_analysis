Music and Simvastatin: Synergistic Effects on Rat Anxiety and Memory
25 sources
·
Jun 27, 2026

Research conducted by Pangemanan et al. (2024) and Sampaio et al. (2017) explores how music therapy, specifically Mozart’s compositions, influences the behavior and biology of rats. The first study demonstrates that combining Mozart K448 with the antidepressant fluoxetine significantly improves depressive-like behavior and body weight in stressed rodents. While this combination was effective, the researchers noted that music alone resulted in the highest melatonin levels, whereas fluoxetine was more effective at lowering corticosterone. Complementary research by Sampaio et al. examined the impact of Mozart’s Sonata for Two Pianos across different life stages, from childhood to old age. Their findings indicate that music exposure can reduce anxiety and depression while enhancing memory retention, though results varied by gender and age. Together, these sources suggest that classical music serves as a potent palliative tool for managing mental health conditions and supporting central nervous system development.
