          Abstract                                                                  
      13                                                                            
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      14  Anxiety and depressive disorders impose a major global burden, motivating interest in non-pharmacological
                                                                                    
      15  interventions that may modulate affective processes. Music exposure has been reported to influence
      16  anxiety- and depression-like behaviors, but preclinical findings are heterogeneous and have not been
                                                                                    
      17  quantitatively synthesized. Most studies also focus on average responses, overlooking behavioral variability.
                                                                                    
      18  We conducted a preregistered systematic review (Open Science Framework registration identifier: mv78t)
                                                                                    
      19  and multilevel meta-analysis of experimental studies testing music exposure in laboratory rodents. Following
                                                                                    
      20  PRISMA and PRISMA-EcoEvo guidelines, we synthesized 298 effect sizes from 20 studies using multilevel
                                                                                    
      21  models that accounted for non-independence among effects. Average and variability effects were quantified
                                                                                    
      22  using the log response ratio (ln𝑅𝑅) and log variability ratio (ln𝑉𝑅), respectively. We also conducted a post hoc
                                                                                    
      23  CRIME-Q appraisal of reporting quality, methodological quality, and risk of bias for all the included
                                                                                    
      24  studies.Music exposure was associated with an average decrease of about 20% in anxiety- and depression-
      25  like behaviors relative to control conditions.. In contrast, music exposure did not produce an overall change
                                                                                    
      26  in behavioral variability. Exploratory moderator analyses suggested context-dependent variability responses:
                                                                                    
      27  behavioral assay type and music meta-genre accounted for comparatively greater heterogeneity in ln𝑉𝑅,
                                                                                    
      28  with anxiety-like outcomes tending to show increased variability and depression-like outcomes tending to
                                                                                    
      29  show reduced variability under music exposure.Critical appraisal identified frequent incomplete reporting of
                                                                                    
      30  sample-size justification, allocation procedures, blinding, attrition, and key features of acoustic delivery and
                                                                                    
      31  control conditions. Music exposure therefore appears to reliably shift average affect-related behavior without
                                                                                    
      32  uniformly altering behavioral dispersion, but inference should be restricted primarily to short-term exposures
      33  in young adult laboratory rodents under controlled conditions.            
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      34  Keywords— anxiety; depression; rodent models; auditory enrichment; stress reactivity;
                                                                                    
      35  behavioral variability; multilevel meta-analysis

          2    Introduction                                                         
      40                                                                            
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      41  Anxiety and depressive disorders are among the most prevalent and disabling psychiatric
                                                                                    
      42  conditions worldwide (Ferrari 2022; Airaksinen et al. 2024). Anxiety disorders are
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      43  characterized by persistent fear, hypervigilance, and heightened stress reactivity, whereas
                                                                                    
      44  depressive disorders involve sustained low mood, anhedonia, and impaired motivation.
                                                                                    
      45  Together, these conditions affect hundreds of millions of people globally, causing a
                                                                                    
      46  substantial economic burden and reducing quality of life (Ferrari 2022; Airaksinen et al.
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      47  2024; Ridley et al. 2020). Their high prevalence and chronic nature have driven extensive
                                                                                    
      48  efforts to understand the neural mechanisms underlying affective dysregulation and to
                                                                                    
      49  develop effective treatments. Pharmacological therapies remain a cornerstone of
                                                                                    
      50  treatment, but their efficacy varies and they are often accompanied by side effects or
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      51  incomplete symptom relief (Boulenger 2004; Kelly and Mezuk 2017). Consequently,
                                                                                    
      52  interest has grown in complementary, non-pharmacological approaches that modulate
                                                                                    
      53  affective states by engaging neural systems involved in emotion regulation and stress
                                                                                    
      54  responsiveness. Among these, music exposure has received substantial attention (Witte et
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      55  al. 2022). In humans, music reliably engages limbic, paralimbic, and cortical networks
                                                                                    
      56  implicated in emotion, reward, and stress regulation, eliciting robust physiological and
                                                                                    
      57  affective responses (Arnold et al. 2024; Koelsch 2014; Levitin and Tirovolas 2009). Clinical
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      58  and experimental studies suggest that music can reduce self-reported anxiety and
                                                                                    
      59  depressive symptoms, at least transiently, and may enhance emotional regulation across
                                                                                    
      60  diverse contexts (Fancourt and Finn 2019; Thoma et al. 2013).             
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 5
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      61  Although the neural mechanisms through which music engages affective and stress-
                                                                                    
      62  related systems are increasingly well characterized, translating these findings into reliable
                                                                                    
      63  therapeutic applications remains challenging (Koelsch 2014; Fancourt et al. 2014).
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      64  Individual differences in musical preference, prior experience, clinical condition, and
                                                                                    
      65  cultural context introduce substantial variability in outcomes. This variability complicates
                                                                                    
      66  assessments of when music exposure can consistently modulate anxiety- and depression-
                                                                                    
      67  related processes (Balkwill and Thompson 1999; McDermott et al. 2016). Preclinical
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      68  animal models provide a valuable framework to address these limitations. Rodent models
                                                                                    
      69  allow precise control over auditory exposure, developmental timing, and environmental
                                                                                    
      70  conditions, while enabling standardized assessments of anxiety- and depression-like
                                                                                    
      71  behaviors. Behavioral assays such as the Elevated Plus Maze, Open Field Test, Light–Dark
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      72  Box, Forced Swim Test, Tail Suspension Test, and Sucrose Preference Test are widely used
                                                                                    
      73  to probe stress reactivity, avoidance behavior, and coping strategies under controlled
                                                                                    
      74  conditions (e.g., Cryan and Holmes 2005; Slattery and Cryan 2012). These models permit
                                                                                    
      75  systematic investigation of how environmental inputs interact with neural and endocrine
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      76  systems implicated in affective regulation, including stress-related and motivational
                                                                                    
      77  pathways (Mcewen et al. 2007; Nestler et al. 2002).                       
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      78  Within this experimental framework, a growing literature has examined how music
                                                                                    
      79  exposure affects rodent behavior. Many studies report reductions in anxiety- or
                                                                                    
      80  depression-like behaviors following music exposure, consistent with the idea that
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      81  temporally patterned auditory stimuli such as music can modulate stress-related
                                                                                    
      82  processes (e.g., Escribano et al. 2014; Chikahisa et al. 2007). However, studies differ in the
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 6
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      83  type of music used, exposure duration and timing, developmental stage at exposure,
                                                                                    
      84  behavioral assays employed, control conditions, and whether behaviors are assessed
                                                                                    
      85  under baseline conditions or following experimentally induced stress (Kühlmann et al.
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      86  2018; Le et al. 2025). Consequently, reported effects vary widely, leaving it unclear
                                                                                    
      87  whether music exposure exerts a generalizable effect on affect-related behavior or
                                                                                    
      88  whether its influence is highly context-dependent. A further limitation of the existing
                                                                                    
      89  literature is its near-exclusive focus on changes in average behavioral responses. Yet
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      90  behavioral variability is itself a biologically meaningful property (Westneat et al. 2015).
                                                                                    
      91  Changes in variance can reflect altered stability, sensitivity to environmental perturbations,
                                                                                    
      92  or heterogeneity in individual stress responsiveness. Environmental manipulations may
                                                                                    
      93  reduce variability by stabilizing behavioral responses or increase variability by amplifying
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      94  individual differences (Sih et al. 2015). Experimental evidence also shows that
                                                                                    
      95  pharmacological exposures can alter behavioral variability itself: psychoactive
                                                                                    
      96  compounds have been shown to suppress differences among individuals in fish behavior,
                                                                                    
      97  including across different social contexts (Polverino et al. 2021; Rafeeq et al. 2026).
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
      98  Whether music exposure similarly affects behavioral variability alongside average behavior
                                                                                    
      99  has not been systematically examined.                                     
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     100  Recent advances in meta-analytic methodology allow a more rigorous synthesis of this
                                                                                    
     101  evidence. Multilevel meta-analytic models accommodate multiple, non-independent
                                                                                    
     102  effect sizes from the same study while appropriately partitioning sources of variance
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     103  (Nakagawa and Santos 2012). Effect size metrics such as the log response ratio (ln𝑅𝑅)
                                                                                    
     104  enable comparison across diverse behavioral measures, whereas variance-based metrics
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 7
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     105  such as the log variability ratio (ln𝑉𝑅) permit explicit evaluation of changes in behavioral
                                                                                    
     106  variability (Nakagawa et al. 2015; Senior et al. 2020). Together, these approaches allow
                                                                                    
     107  researchers to assess both the average effects of music exposure on affect-related
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     108  behavior and the consistency of those effects across individuals and experimental
                                                                                    
     109  contexts.                                                                 
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     110  Here, we present a preregistered systematic review and multilevel meta-analysis of
                                                                                    
     111  experimental studies examining how music exposure affects anxiety- and depression-like
                                                                                    
     112  behaviors in laboratory rodents. We focused on laboratory mice and rats experimentally
                                                                                    
     113  exposed to music and compared with concurrent non-music controls, with eligibility
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     114  restricted to studies reporting standardized anxiety- or depression-like behavioral
                                                                                    
     115  outcomes. Reported in accordance with PRISMA and PRISMA-EcoEvo guidelines (O’Dea et
                                                                                    
     116  al. 2021; Page et al. 2021), this synthesis quantifies both average behavioral effects and
                                                                                    
     117  changes in behavioral variability, identifies sources of heterogeneity, and evaluates the
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     118  robustness of reported effects. By clarifying the magnitude, consistency, and context
                                                                                    
     119  dependence of music-induced behavioral changes under controlled conditions, this study
                                                                                    
     120  provides a quantitative and conceptual framework for interpreting preclinical evidence
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     121  relevant to affective regulation and hypotheses about sensory modulation.

     123  3.1  Data overview                                                        
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     124  Twenty studies met the predefined inclusion criteria (Flores-Gutiérrez et al. 2018; Fu et al.
                                                                                    
     125  2025, 2023; Sampaio et al. 2017; Chikahisa et al. 2007; Papadakakis et al. 2019; Escribano
                                                                                    
     126  et al. 2014; Saghari et al. 2021; Krishnamurthy and Rao 2025; Camargo et al. 2013; Li et al.
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     127  2010; Rizzolo et al. 2021; Uşak et al. 2020; Freitas Oliveira et al. 2020; Cheng et al. 2024;
                                                                                    
     128  Milbratz de Camargo et al. 2017; Ren and Lu 2024; Chen et al. 2019; Pangemanan et al.
                                                                                    
     129  2024; Niehues da Cruz et al. 2011), yielding 298 effect sizes for meta-analysis. Study
                                                                                    
     130  selection and exclusion decisions are summarized in the PRISMA flow diagram (Fig. 1).
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     131  Reported sample sizes were generally modest, averaging 𝑛 = 9.76 animals in control
                                                                                    
     132  conditions (SD = 2.53; median = 10) and 𝑛 = 9.81 animals in music-exposed conditions
                                                                                    
     133  (SD = 2.59; median = 10), with study-level average sample sizes ranging from 6 to 14
                                                                                    
     134  animals per condition. These values represent the numbers of animals contributing to the
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     135  reported outcomes rather than necessarily the number of independently replicated
                                                                                    
     136  exposure units. Most effect sizes were derived from anxiety-like outcomes (77%, 𝑛 = 231),
                                                                                    
     137  followed by depression-like outcomes (𝑛 = 64), with three effect sizes classified as
                                                                                    
     138  representing both outcome types. Effect sizes were nearly evenly split between mice (Mus
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     139  musculus, 48%, four strains) and rats (Rattus norvegicus, 52%, two strains; Fig. 2A), with
                                                                                    
     140  all analyzed comparisons derived from wild-type or otherwise non-genetically modified
                                                                                    
     141  animals. Tested animals were predominantly male (60%), followed by female (38%) and
                                                                                    
     142  mixed-sex cohorts (2%). This imbalance was especially pronounced for depression-like

          4    Discussion                                                           
     298                                                                            
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     299  Music exposure reduced anxiety- and depression-like behaviors by about 20% relative to
                                                                                    
     300  control conditions across experimental contexts, as indicated by a significantly negative
                                                                                    
     301  overall ln𝑅𝑅 (Fig. 3A). This reduction occurred across both anxiety- and depression-like
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     302  outcomes (Fig. 4A) and across life stages at exposure (Fig. 5A), indicating that the overall
                                                                                    
     303  direction of the association was generally consistent across these broad categories,
                                                                                    
     304  despite substantial heterogeneity among effect sizes. However, the magnitude of the
                                                                                    
     305  average response differed among behavioral assays, indicating that the estimated effect of
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     306  music also depended on how affect-related behavior was measured. Although effect sizes
                                                                                    
     307  showed substantial heterogeneity (Table 1), the predominance of negative effect sizes
                                                                                    
     308  supports a clear directional effect. Music exposure did not systematically alter behavioral
                                                                                    
     309  variability. The overall lnVR estimate was close to zero and non-significant (Fig. 3B),
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     310  showing that music exposure neither consistently reduced nor increased behavioral
                                                                                    
     311  dispersion. In other words, music exposure shifted average affect-related behavior without
                                                                                    
     312  broadly homogenizing responses or amplifying differences between individuals. This
                                                                                    
     313  distinction highlights the conceptual difference between average effects (lnRR), which
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 21
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     314  capture shifts in average behavior, and variability effects (lnVR), which capture changes in
                                                                                    
     315  dispersion (Nakagawa et al. 2015). The absence of a global lnVR effect indicates that
                                                                                    
     316  music alters central tendency without uniformly reshaping individual variation. Exploratory
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     317  outcome-specific analyses suggested different variability patterns for anxiety- and
                                                                                    
     318  depression-like outcomes: variability tended to increase for anxiety-like behaviors and
                                                                                    
     319  decrease for depression-like behaviors. However, the depression-specific evidence was
                                                                                    
     320  strongly male biased, with female outcomes represented by only six effect sizes from two
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     321  studies, so these differences should be interpreted cautiously. Moderator analyses also
                                                                                    
     322  revealed potential heterogeneity in variability responses across behavioral assays, music
                                                                                    
     323  meta-genres, and experimental designs (Fig. 6B–C). The estimated strain-level variance
                                                                                    
     324  component was close to zero in both average and variability models. However, only six
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     325  strains were represented in the dataset, and two of these occurred in a single study each.
                                                                                    
     326  The near-zero estimate should therefore not be interpreted as evidence that strain has no
                                                                                    
     327  biological influence on behavioral responses; rather, the available evidence provides
                                                                                    
     328  limited information for estimating strain-level heterogeneity. This is particularly important
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     329  because strain differences are known to influence behavioral outcomes (Russo et al. 2012)
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     330  4.1  Music exposure in the context of environmental enrichment            
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     331  The direction of these average effects aligns with findings from other non-pharmacological
                                                                                    
     332  interventions, particularly voluntary exercise and environmental enrichment. It is also
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     333  broadly consistent with evidence from human studies reporting reductions in anxiety and
                                                                                    
     334  depressive symptoms following music-based interventions (Witte et al. 2022), although the
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 22
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     335  present results concern experimentally controlled exposure in laboratory rodents and
                                                                                    
     336  should not be interpreted as direct evidence of therapeutic efficacy in humans. Exercise
                                                                                    
     337  reliably reduces immobility in stress-coping assays and increases exploratory behavior in
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     338  anxiety-related tests (Duman et al. 2008; Mul and Mul 2018; Brenes et al. 2020; Yang et al.
                                                                                    
     339  2025). Although we cannot directly compare effect magnitudes across paradigms, the
                                                                                    
     340  shared direction of effects suggests that music functions as a sensory form of
                                                                                    
     341  environmental enrichment rather than as a pharmacological analogue. Music exposure
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     342  produced stronger reductions in induced paradigms than in assays measuring baseline
                                                                                    
     343  affective behavior (Fig. 5D). Induced models (e.g., chronic stress or learned helplessness)
                                                                                    
     344  elevate anxiety- and depression-like phenotypes above typical levels (Mineur et al. 2006),
                                                                                    
     345  creating greater scope for behavioral improvement. Interventions that modulate stress
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     346  physiology should therefore produce larger effects under heightened disturbance than
                                                                                    
     347  under baseline conditions, where responses already fall within a restricted range.
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     348  Timing also influenced effect magnitude. Studies that delivered music prior to behavioral
                                                                                    
     349  testing showed larger reductions in anxiety- and depression-like behaviors than studies
                                                                                    
     350  presenting music concurrently with testing. Rather than contradicting evidence for acute
                                                                                    
     351  music-related changes in arousal or cognition (Rickard et al. 2005), this pattern suggests
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     352  that music primarily shifts baseline affective states rather than enhancing task
                                                                                    
     353  performance. When introduced during testing, music may act as an additional sensory
                                                                                    
     354  stimulus that interacts with task demands, whereas prior exposure allows physiological
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     355  and affective modulation to occur before assessment. Several mechanisms may account
                                                                                    
     356  for these patterns. Music may modulate stress physiology through the hypothalamic–
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 23
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     357  pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis, reducing stress reactivity and downstream glucocorticoid
                                                                                    
     358  signaling (Smith and Vale 2006; Mcewen et al. 2007). Alternatively, music may increase
                                                                                    
     359  environmental predictability. Chronic unpredictable mild stress reliably induces anxiety-
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     360  and depression-like phenotypes (Willner 2017), whereas music exposure typically occurs
                                                                                    
     361  in stable, repeatable contexts. By increasing sensory predictability, music may dampen
                                                                                    
     362  stress responses associated with uncertainty. This interpretation aligns with the joint
                                                                                    
     363  ln𝑅𝑅–ln𝑉𝑅 pattern: music consistently shifts average behavior while altering variability
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     364  only in specific contexts. As with other enrichment-based interventions, heterogeneity is
                                                                                    
     365  expected rather than anomalous (Würbel 2001; Simpson and Kelly 2011).     
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     366  4.2  Assay-, stimulus-, and design-specific variability                   
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     367  Behavioral assays strongly moderated variability responses. Relative to the Elevated Plus
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     368  Maze (EPM), music exposure reduced variability in the Forced Swim Test and Tail
                                                                                    
     369  Suspension Test (Fig. 6A). These assays constrain animals to a limited behavioral
                                                                                    
     370  repertoire and probe stress-coping states (Cryan and Holmes 2005; Leite-Almeida et al.
                                                                                    
     371  2022). Under such constraints, music appears to promote convergence toward similar
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     372  coping responses, reducing behavioral dispersion across animals. In contrast, music
                                                                                    
     373  exposure increased variability in the EPM. The EPM assesses approach–avoidance conflict
                                                                                    
     374  and typically produces substantial individual variability (Walf and Frye 2007; Knight et al.
                                                                                    
     375  2021; Albani et al. 2015). Music may amplify pre-existing differences in how animals
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     376  balance exploration and avoidance, increasing dispersion even when average anxiety-like
                                                                                    
     377  behavior declines. Increased variability in this context does not imply adverse effects;
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 24
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     378  rather, it reflects the sensitivity of this behavioral domain to individual traits and assay
                                                                                    
     379  structure.                                                                
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     380  These findings carry practical implications. Researchers who prioritize reproducibility and
                                                                                    
     381  precise estimation of average effects may obtain more consistent outcomes using assays
                                                                                    
     382  that exhibit lower variability under music exposure, such as the Forced Swim Test or Tail
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     383  Suspension Test. In contrast, assays like the EPM may require larger samples or explicit
                                                                                    
     384  modeling of heterogeneity to achieve comparable precision. No assay is inherently
                                                                                    
     385  superior; instead, tasks differ in how consistently animals respond under identical auditory
                                                                                    
     386  conditions.                                                               
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     387  Music meta-genre also structured variability. Studies using popular contemporary and
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     388  traditional or folk music showed greater dispersion than studies using mixed stimuli,
                                                                                    
     389  whereas Western art or orchestral music did not differ significantly from the reference
                                                                                    
     390  category (Fig. 6C). Although genre categories are coarse, they likely capture differences in
                                                                                    
     391  auditory temporal structure and predictability. Predictable auditory patterns tend to elicit
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     392  more consistent neural responses (Heilbron and Chait 2018; Auksztulewicz et al. 2017),
                                                                                    
     393  whereas less predictable rhythms amplify differential processing and expectation
                                                                                    
     394  violations (Foldal et al. 2020). Thus, genre-dependent variability likely reflects differences
                                                                                    
     395  in how consistently animals encode and integrate auditory input.          
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     396  Experimental design also influenced variability. Posttest-only designs were associated
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     397  with greater behavioral dispersion under music exposure than factorial or repeated-
                                                                                    
     398  measures designs (Fig. 6D), as well as more negative average effects relative to factorial
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 25
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     399  designs. Thus, studies using posttest-only designs tended to report larger reductions in
                                                                                    
     400  anxiety- and depression-like behavior, but also a greater music–control difference in
                                                                                    
     401  behavioral variability. One possible explanation is that more structured designs can better
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     402  isolate music-specific effects from variation associated with baseline states, time, co-
                                                                                    
     403  manipulations, or repeated testing. By contrast, posttest-only comparisons may retain
                                                                                    
     404  these unmeasured sources of variation within the observed treatment contrast.
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     405  Design architecture may therefore affect both the estimated average effect and the
                                                                                    
     406  apparent dispersion response, rather than merely acting as a neutral feature of study
                                                                                    
     407  implementation. However, this moderator pattern should not be interpreted causally;
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     408  design architecture is likely confounded by other experimental features, including the
                                                                                    
     409  choice of induced model, exposure protocol, and behavioral assay. Nevertheless, it
                                                                                    
     410  indicates that some variability attributed to music exposure may reflect differences in
                                                                                    
     411  experimental design rather than genuine biological heterogeneity in individual responses
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     412  alone.                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     413  4.3  Heterogeneity, reporting practices, and robustness                   
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     414  The combined ln𝑅𝑅 and ln𝑉𝑅 results indicate substantial heterogeneity in how music
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     415  exposure was implemented and experienced across studies (Kühlmann et al. 2018; Le et al.
                                                                                    
     416  2025). Differences in genre, duration, timing, acoustic delivery, control conditions, and
                                                                                    
     417  experimental context are likely to interact in non-additive ways, complicating mechanistic
                                                                                    
     418  inference. Music meta-genre explained little heterogeneity in average effects but
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 26
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     419  substantial heterogeneity in behavioral variability, indicating that average outcomes alone
                                                                                    
     420  can obscure important dimensions of behavioral sensitivity.               
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     421  The CRIME-Q assessment (Fig. [fig]) is most informative not as an inventory of missing
                                                                                    
     422  information, but as a limit on causal attribution. In an acoustic intervention, the nominal
                                                                                    
     423  stimulus does not fully define the treatment received by the animals. Sound level and
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     424  frequency spectrum, speaker placement, room acoustics, cage position, background
                                                                                    
     425  noise, group housing, and separation from control animals can all modify effective
                                                                                    
     426  exposure. Ambient acoustic conditions can vary substantially among rooms and cages,
                                                                                    
     427  and differences in acoustic context can alter rodent behavior even when other
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     428  experimental procedures are similar (Prior 2006; Barabas et al. 2022). Consequently,
                                                                                    
     429  incomplete reporting of exposure delivery and control conditions makes it difficult to
                                                                                    
     430  determine whether differences among studies reflect the musical stimulus itself, total
                                                                                    
     431  acoustic stimulation, or broader features of the housing environment.     
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     432  Similarly, unclear ratings for baseline balance, allocation, housing, and blinding do not
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     433  demonstrate that the studies were biased; rather, they limit our ability to evaluate the
                                                                                    
     434  direction and magnitude of any bias (Hooijmans et al. 2014; Andersen et al. 2024). A more
                                                                                    
     435  specific concern emerged for treatment-level replication. Our review of the original
                                                                                    
     436  exposure protocols showed that music was usually delivered to several animals sharing a
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     437  cage or room, whereas primary analyses generally treated individual animals as
                                                                                    
     438  independent replicates. In such designs, the cage or room rather than the individual
                                                                                    
     439  animal may constitute the independently replicated treatment unit. Because our sampling
                                                                                    
     440  variances were calculated from the animal sample sizes reported by the original studies,
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 27
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     441  any unaccounted cage- or room-level clustering is inherited by the meta-analysis. Where
                                                                                    
     442  the number of animals exceeds the number of independent acoustic exposure units,
                                                                                    
     443  sampling variances may therefore be underestimated and individual effect sizes may
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     444  receive greater precision than warranted. Cage- and room-level replication was not
                                                                                    
     445  reported consistently enough to reconstruct effective sample sizes retrospectively, so this
                                                                                    
     446  limitation could not be corrected quantitatively. Thus, the observed heterogeneity likely
                                                                                    
     447  reflects a combination of biological differences, methodological variation, and differences
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     448  in experimental structure and precision across studies rather than any single source.
                                                                                    
     449  Despite these limitations, multiple diagnostics support the robustness of the overall
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     450  average effect: we found no evidence of small-study or time-lag bias, and leave-one-out
                                                                                    
     451  analyses showed that no individual study drove the estimated effect. These diagnostics
                                                                                    
     452  indicate that the pooled result was not attributable to a single influential study or a clear
                                                                                    
     453  publication-related artifact, although they do not address the treatment-level replication
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     454  issue described above. Generalization should nevertheless be restricted to the
                                                                                    
     455  experimental contexts represented in the literature. Most studies involved young adult
                                                                                    
     456  laboratory mice or rats exposed to music over relatively short periods under controlled
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     457  housing conditions. The estimated ≈ 20% reduction in anxiety- and depression-like
                                                                                    
     458  behaviors should therefore be interpreted as applying primarily to comparable laboratory
                                                                                    
     459  settings. Extrapolation to other developmental stages, longer exposures, other species, or
                                                                                    
     460  more complex environments requires direct testing.                        
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 28
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     461  Other design features also remain insufficiently characterized. Reporting of the light–dark
                                                                                    
     462  phase during music exposure or behavioral testing was uncommon, limiting assessment of
                                                                                    
     463  whether circadian timing contributed to variation in either average effects or behavioral
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     464  variability (Saré et al. 2021). In addition, most primary studies were designed to detect
                                                                                    
     465  average differences rather than changes in behavioral variability. The context-dependent
                                                                                    
     466  ln𝑉𝑅 patterns should therefore be interpreted cautiously, particularly given the smaller
                                                                                    
     467  evidence base and exploratory nature of the moderator analyses.           
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     468  4.4  Study-specific limitations and recommendations for future            
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     469  research                                                                  
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     470  The CRIME-Q assessment identifies several priorities for future research on music
                                                                                    
     471  exposure and affect-related behavior. Studies should report music and control conditions
                                                                                    
     472  in sufficient detail to permit replication, including the stimulus identity or composition,
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     473  delivery hardware, sound level, speaker–cage arrangement, exposure schedule, acoustic
                                                                                    
     474  separation between treatment and control animals, and whether exposure was delivered
                                                                                    
     475  at the cage, room, or animal level. Reporting the light–dark phase of exposure and testing,
                                                                                    
     476  habituation procedures, housing conditions, and cage or room allocation would further
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     477  clarify potential circadian and environmental sources of variation. Future experiments
                                                                                    
     478  should also strengthen safeguards against bias by reporting a priori sample-size
                                                                                    
     479  calculations, explicit allocation methods, baseline group characteristics, housing or
                                                                                    
     480  position randomization, blinding of handlers and outcome assessors where feasible, and
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     481  animal-level attrition and exclusions by treatment group. Prespecifying outcomes and
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 29
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     482  making protocols or analysis plans available would improve the assessment of selective
                                                                                    
     483  reporting. Finally, studies should be designed to assess both average responses and
                                                                                    
     484  behavioral variability. This will require larger samples, repeated-measures designs where
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     485  appropriate, and reporting of individual-level data or response distributions rather than
                                                                                    
     486  group summaries alone. The experimental unit should be specified explicitly, with cage- or
                                                                                    
     487  room-level replication and hierarchical analyses used when exposure or housing is shared
                                                                                    
     488  among animals. Together, these improvements would allow future work to distinguish
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     489  more clearly between music-specific effects, broader acoustic or housing effects, and
                                                                                    
     490  genuine differences in individual behavioral responsiveness.              
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     491  4.5  Conclusions                                                          
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     492  This meta-analysis shows that music exposure is associated, on average, with an
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     493  approximately 20% reduction in anxiety- and depression-like behaviors in laboratory
                                                                                    
     494  rodents, while effects on behavioral variability are strongly context dependent. Music
                                                                                    
     495  exposure primarily shifts the central tendency of affect-related behavior, with changes in
                                                                                    
     496  behavioral dispersion emerging only under specific experimental and stimulus conditions.
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     497  Joint consideration of average effects and variability therefore provides a more complete
                                                                                    
     498  framework for interpreting heterogeneous experimental results and for evaluating sensory
                                                                                    
     499  and environmental interventions.

     573  We implemented six refinements to our protocol after preregistration. To accommodate
                                                                                    
     574  heterogeneity in reporting practices, we introduced the first three refinements during data

     576  adjustments did not alter the PECO framework, eligibility criteria, outcome definitions, or
                                                                                    
     577  primary analytical strategy.                                              
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     578  First, to model statistical dependence among effect sizes, we coded the comparison
                                                                                    
     579  structure of each effect size as either independent or dependent (within-subject/repeated-
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     580  measures). This expansion of our preregistered metadata structure allowed for accurate
                                                                                    
     581  sampling-variance specification when a single experimental cohort yielded multiple effect
                                                                                    
     582  sizes.

     584  short-term, medium-term, or long-term) and grouped music stimuli into broad meta-
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     585  genres based on historical context and dominant musical features (see Table S1 for
                                                                                    
     586  definitions). We derived these classifications from the reported text to facilitate
                                                                                    
     587  interpretable moderator analyses.                                         
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     588  Third, we recorded whether studies explicitly reported key stimulus parameters, such as
                                                                                    
     589  loudness, frequency, and light–dark phases. Because loudness and frequency were

     591  treating them as missing data, allowing us to quantify reporting gaps.

     684  5.6  Meta-analytic models                                                 
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     685  We fitted multilevel meta-analytic models separately for lnRR and lnVR using restricted
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     686  maximum likelihood (REML) estimation in metafor (Viechtbauer 2010). Because individual
                                                                                    
     687  studies often contributed multiple, non-independent effect sizes (e.g., multiple outcomes,
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 40
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     688  assays, or contrasts from the same experimental cohort), we explicitly modeled
                                                                                    
     689  dependence among effect sizes in two ways. First, sampling covariances were
                                                                                    
     690  incorporated using a variance–covariance matrix (𝑽) constructed at the cohort level, such
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     691  that effect sizes derived from the same cohort were assumed to have correlated sampling
                                                                                    
     692  errors (with 𝜌 = 0.5 when within-cohort correlations were not reported), yielding a block-
                                                                                    
     693  diagonal-structured 𝑽 matrix (Noble et al. 2017; Nakagawa et al. 2022). Second, we
                                                                                    
     694  included random intercepts for study ID to account for between-study heterogeneity, for
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     695  effect size ID to accommodate residual within-study variation among effect sizes beyond
                                                                                    
     696  sampling error, and for strain to control for systematic differences among rodent strains.
                                                                                    
     697  Moderator analyses were conducted as separate uni-moderator meta-regressions using
                                                                                    
     698  the same random-effects structure and the same 𝑽 matrix as the corresponding overall
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     699  model. Given the number of moderators and pairwise contrasts relative to the size of the
                                                                                    
     700  evidence base, these analyses were treated as exploratory. We did not apply a formal
                                                                                    
     701  multiple-comparison correction because stringent family-wise error corrections can
                                                                                    
     702  substantially reduce statistical power and increase the risk of false-negative results when
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     703  many related hypotheses are evaluated (Pike 2011; Drezner and Drezner 2016, Gelman et
                                                                                    
     704  al. 2012). Instead, we considered the magnitude and uncertainty of estimated effects
                                                                                    
     705  together with the proportion of heterogeneity explained by each moderator, and
                                                                                    
     706  interpreted individual contrasts as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     707  Inference was based on 𝑡-tests as implemented in metafor, and all models were fitted
                                                                                    
     708  using REML.                                                               
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 41

     728  5.8  Small-study effects, time-lag patterns, and sensitivity analyzes     
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     729  We assessed potential small-study effects and time-lag patterns using complementary
                                                                                    
     730  visual and model-based approaches appropriate for multilevel meta-analyzes (Nakagawa
                                                                                    
     731  et al. 2022). These analyzes were treated as diagnostic tools and interpreted cautiously
                                                                                    
     732  given the substantial heterogeneity and non-independence of effect sizes. First, we visually
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     733  inspected funnel plots for asymmetry using effect sizes from the fitted multilevel meta-
                                                                                    
     734  analytic model, plotted against measures of precision (standard error and inverse standard
                                                                                    
     735  error). Visual inspection was used to assess whether less precise studies showed
                                                                                    
     736  systematic deviations from the overall pattern. Second, we evaluated small-study effects
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     737  using an Egger-type regression adapted for multilevel meta-analytic models. In classical
                                                                                    
     738  Egger’s regression, effect sizes are regressed on their sampling standard errors (Egger et al.
                                                                                    
     739  1997). However, for the log response ratio (ln𝑅𝑅), effect sizes and sampling variances are
                                                                                    
     740  inherently correlated, which can bias inference. To avoid this issue, we used the effective
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     741  sample size:                                                              
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                      1    1   1                                    
     742                            √  = √  +   ,                                   
                                     𝑁̃   𝑁   𝑁                                     
                                           𝑇   𝐶                                    
                                                                                    
     743  where 𝑁 and 𝑁 are the treatment and control sample sizes, respectively. This term was
                𝑇    𝐶                                                              
     744  included as a moderator in multilevel meta-regression models with the same random-
                                                                                    
     745  effects structure as the primary analyzes. Third, we examined potential time-lag (decline)
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 43
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     746  effects by including mean-centered publication year as a moderator. To disentangle
                                                                                    
     747  temporal trends from small-study effects, we fitted both uni-moderator models (including
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     748  either publication year or                                                
                            √1/𝑁̃                                                   
                                ) and a multi-moderator model including both predictors
     749  simultaneously. All models followed the same variance–covariance structure and random-
     750  effects specification as the primary meta-analyzes (Nakagawa et al. 2022). Finally, we
                                                                                    
     751  assessed the influence of individual studies using a leave-one-out sensitivity analysis.
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     752  Each study was sequentially removed from the dataset, and the multilevel meta-analytic
                                                                                    
     753  model was refitted to evaluate the stability of the overall effect estimate. This analysis was
                                                                                    
     754  used to identify whether any single study exerted disproportionate influence on the
                                                                                    
     755  estimated average effect or its uncertainty (Nakagawa, Yang, et al. 2023).
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     756  5.9  Critical appraisal of reporting, methodological quality, and risk of 
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     757  bias                                                                      
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     758  After the primary analyses, we conducted a post hoc critical appraisal using an adapted
                                                                                    
     759  version of the Critical Appraisal of Methodological (technical) Quality, Quality of Reporting
                                                                                    
     760  and Risk of Bias in Animal Research (CRIME-Q) framework (Andersen et al. 2024). We
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     761  assessed 20 reports at the study level using 20 items across three domains: quality of
                                                                                    
     762  reporting (QoR), methodological quality (MQ), and risk of bias (RoB). We omitted CRIME-Q
                                                                                    
     763  bench-top or laboratory-procedure items that were not applicable to these whole-animal
                                                                                    
     764  music-exposure experiments.                                               
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 44
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     765  QoR items evaluated whether reports provided sufficient detail for replication and
                                                                                    
     766  appraisal, including peer-review status, animal characteristics, music-exposure
                                                                                    
     767  procedures, animal-welfare compliance, blinding, methods–results congruency, study
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     768  limitations, and conflicts of interest. MQ items evaluated whether the animals and music-
                                                                                    
     769  exposure design were appropriate for the stated behavioral question, including group
                                                                                    
     770  comparability, sample-size justification, acoustic delivery, exposure fidelity, control
                                                                                    
     771  conditions, and potential acoustic cross-contamination. RoB items examined baseline
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     772  balance (whether relevant pre-intervention animal or group characteristics were
                                                                                    
     773  comparable between music and control groups), allocation sequence generation, housing
                                                                                    
     774  or cage-position effects, outcome-assessment selection rules, experimenter or handler
                                                                                    
     775  blinding, outcome-assessor blinding, attrition, selective outcome reporting, and potential
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     776  funder or commercial influence.                                           
                                                                                    
     777  Each item was scored using its prespecified CRIME-Q response options. Reporting and
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     778  methodological-quality items were generally scored as Yes, Partly, or No; risk-of-bias
                                                                                    
     779  items were generally scored as Yes, No, or Unclear, with Not applicable available only for
                                                                                    
     780  items where the codebook allowed it. We retained ratings as item-level categorical data
                                                                                    
     781  and did not combine them into an overall quality score. S.O. performed the initial appraisal
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     782  using the original reports and a study-specific CRIME-Q coding manual. NotebookLM (3.5
                                                                                    
     783  Flash) was then used as a source-grounded extraction aid to cross-check each item
                                                                                    
     784  against the primary studies, the adapted CRIME-Q codebook, and the coding rules. The AI
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     785  output was treated as provisional: all discrepancies and uncertain ratings were resolved by
                                                                                    
     786  rechecking the original sources. Finally, A.L. independently cross-checked a random
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
### PAGE 45
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     787  sample of two studies (10% of reports). The adapted CRIME-Q codebook, including the
                                                                                    
     788  item definitions, response options, and operational decision rules used in the appraisal, is
                                                                                    
     789  provided in Supplementary Material. The NotebookLM prompts and instructions,
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     790  supporting workflow files, and finalized study-level assessment matrix are available in the
